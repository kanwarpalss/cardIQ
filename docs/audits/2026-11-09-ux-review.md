# CardIQ — UX and information hierarchy review

**Agent:** Codex · **Date:** 9 November 2026

KP requested a complete space and usability pass while retaining the new visual theme. The governing choice is progressive disclosure: show the decision or useful record first, then let the user open evidence, explanations and maintenance tools. No payment, transfer, reward, parser, sync or database calculation changed in this pass.

## Adversarial review and tradeoffs

- The load-bearing assumption is that infrequent explanations and maintenance tools should not compete with daily scanning. Every folded section has a labelled summary; financial warnings, active filter state and expiry remain visible.
- Keeping standalone Insights, stacking all three transfer views and permanently showing every route explanation were rejected because they recreate duplication and long pages.
- Mounted hidden exploration views deliberately preserve calculator/search/filter/table state. Trends loads on first use, then remains mounted while switching within Spend. Main navigation retains its existing tab lifetime.
- “Best ratio” is a points conversion comparison, not a claim that different currencies have equal cash value. Ties, disputed ratios, source dates, fees and caps retain their existing meaning and calculation source.
- Card images are public current issuer product artwork bundled locally; older issued generations or networks may look different. See [asset provenance](../../public/cards/README.md). Account names and last four remain outside the decorative stock image.
- This is a presentation/navigation review. Existing source-data gaps and statement reconciliation work remain separate. No user financial records or private screenshots are included in this document or Git.
